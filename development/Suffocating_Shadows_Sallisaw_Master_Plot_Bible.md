# THE SUFFOCATING SHADOWS OF SALLISAW
## The Chosen One — Master Plot Blueprint, Version 1
### A psychological hacking thriller and a love story about a father fighting to save his family

**Status:** New, editable story architecture. This is a proposed *fictional novel*, not a verified account of real agencies, real people, or real events. The original GitHub manuscript has **not** been rewritten or changed.

**Author's working name:** Skyler Blue  
**Working protagonist spelling:** Skyler Blue (standardize from Skylar/Skyler throughout)  
**Primary setting:** Sallisaw, Oklahoma, and fictional nearby towns, offices, churches and facilities  
**Form:** Prologue + 36 chapters in six acts + epilogue + optional private family letters  
**Target:** 112,000–125,000 words; full-length adult thriller, 3,000–3,400 words per main chapter on average  
**Narrative:** Close third-person predominantly from Skyler; a few carefully marked Lena, Mara, Kaylee and Dan scenes only when they strengthen dramatic irony without spoiling the twist.

> **The contract with the reader:** Something criminal really is happening in this fictional world, but *not everything that frightens Skyler comes from the criminals*. Evidence matters; coincidence remains coincidence; Skyler can be misled and can make mistakes. The plot rewards verification, accountability, love and difficult choices—not certainty without proof.

# 1. THE STORY'S ENGINE

## One-sentence premise
A gifted coder who loses his fiancée and nearly loses his daughters after moving to Sallisaw must use his privacy-first ShadowChat platform and his unfinished SkyCoin project to expose an influence-for-profit network, only to learn that the retired FBI director who keeps rescuing him designed the traps—and that proving it may endanger the very family he is trying to protect.

## The emotional promise
This opens like *Romeo and Juliet*, with a boy convinced love is defeated by outsiders. It ends by overturning that belief: real love also demands honesty, consent, restraint and care. Skyler saves his family not by becoming a mythic “Chosen One” or winning Kaylee back as a prize, but by protecting his daughters, telling the truth about his own choices, and building a safe co-parenting future with Kaylee. New romance remains possible; it does not replace fatherhood.

## Central dramatic question
Can Skyler prove a specific conspiracy before a staged drug-and-trafficking case and a forged online confession destroy his custody prospects, reputation and family's safety—without exposing his children's private lives to the world?

## Plot machinery — one coherent conspiracy, not ten unrelated villains
**The Society** is a fictional influence-and-extortion circle, half elite club and half coercive cult, hidden inside a philanthropic “family safety” network. It is not the FBI or a foreign government as a whole. Its criminal operations are privately funded and carried out by a small group of bad actors, contractors and opportunists.

- **The Choir:** Buys promotion, remixes recordings, places targeted audio and posts, and manufactures the appearance of an American-versus-Chinese rap battle. It cannot read minds or control every song.
- **The Mirror Room:** Profiles people from purchased data and misused vendor permissions; generates multiple possible stories about a target and tests which messages provoke reactions.
- **The Ledger:** Routes extortion proceeds and hush payments through sham charities, bogus contracts, and promotional crypto wallets. It does not require the entire cryptocurrency market to be rigged.
- **The Pawns:** Recruit ordinary people—some knowingly, some fooled—to make calls, relay screenshots, deliver packages, or pressure employers, churches and relationships.
- **Dan Mercer (fictional):** Publicly benevolent retired FBI director in an alternate fictional setting. The mastermind is *one retired official abusing reputation and connections*, not a factual allegation against the real FBI. He invented a “rescue-and-recruit” cycle: put Skyler in danger, then arrive as his savior and ask for access.
- **Vera Cross (fictional):** An influential executive several levels above Skyler. Her mixture of attraction, resentment and desire for control provides an inside route to his job, prototypes and professional identity. Her choices create independent wrongdoing, not merely an extension of Dan.
- **Grant (fictional):** Kaylee's wealthy, charming stepfather. An early suspect because he hired a private family-safety consultant. His real role is limited and morally complicated: he paid for monitoring in the name of protecting family, but did **not** secretly pay police to frame Skyler. His vendor's information reached Dan's circle. This false assumption becomes a crucial correction.

## Exactly what the criminals want
1. Obtain administrative access to **ShadowChat's old consent/identity prototype**, whose audit design can establish which private contractor bought and misused identity data. The new ShadowChat itself is the hero's tool, not an omnipotent weapon.
2. Control or discredit Skyler before he can testify about the prototype's sale, leaked permissions and the **Elias Vale family-protection trust**.
3. Trick him into signing a $10 million “online operations” contract that can be edited into evidence of a drug-trafficking enterprise; failing that, push him into panicked acts that can be portrayed as violence or fraud.
4. Extort money or access to the legally established trust for Skyler's daughters while preserving Dan's image as the man who protected the family.

## The inheritance, made narratively credible
**Elias Vale** is the fictional world's wealthiest technology founder. Years ago his company acquired a firm that mishandled an early Aegis/ShadowChat identity design. When Vale's independent investigators find evidence of misuse, his legal office creates a **protected settlement-and-education trust** for Skyler's children and funds counsel. The trust is real and legally administered; Skyler cannot simply withdraw an enormous sum. Its records, however, could expose the vendor chain. An impostor extortionist demands millions to suppress its existence. Dan spreads a baseless rumor pinning the money on a real-famous tech billionaire; the story later explicitly disproves that rumor. For a publication-safe final edition, keep both billionaires fictional.

## First undeniable event, midpoint, twist, climax
- **First proof (Ch. 6):** Independent security footage and physical inspection establish an unauthorized entry into the rented family home; this proves *a trespass*, not the culprit or every earlier online suspicion.
- **Midpoint (Ch. 18):** Laboratory review of the counterfeit-money bait and a subpoenaed contractor invoice connect the staged cartel offer, device compromise and Vale trust to one private vendor.
- **Darkest moment (Ch. 30):** Haley admits she knowingly relayed confidential material to a “protection liaison”; Kaylee receives a forged custody-risk alert; a suspicious figure is caught on a neighbor's camera near the girls' school. Dan offers to make it all disappear if Skyler signs over access.
- **Enemy in plain sight (Ch. 32):** Dan's supposedly helpful “rescue” paperwork contains the authorization chain for the same contractor operations Skyler was fleeing. Mara corroborates it with authenticated records and a witness.
- **Climax (Chs. 34–35):** Dan tries to activate a prewritten trafficking confession while using the family as leverage. Skyler refuses a public spectacle, protects the girls, secures independent witnesses, and publishes only the verified, redacted record. Dan's protection collapses under lawful scrutiny, but the broader network is not magically eliminated.
- **Final victory (Ch. 36 and Epilogue):** The immediate threat to the family ends, the manufactured case falls apart, the girls' trust is protected, and Skyler and Kaylee establish reliable co-parenting. This is *saving the family* without forcing a romantic reunion.

# 2. PEOPLE AND LOVE-STORY DESIGN

## The family
**Skyler Blue:** Brilliant, ambitious, funny, impulsive coder. His flaw is trying to solve emotional pain like a technical problem and treating every coincidence as a clue when frightened. His strength is learning to ask for independent verification—and to protect people instead of making them witnesses for his case.

**Kaylee:** Skyler's first enduring love, former fiancée and mother of Luna Avigail, Summer Skye and Alexis Isabella-Jane. After losing her mother, she needs stability and resents Skyler's secrecy, workplace complications and fixation on his phone. She is neither an automatic accomplice nor a reward he wins. Her defining choice is to trust verifiable evidence and cooperate to keep their children safe without forgetting the hurt between them.

**Luna Avigail:** The chess knight—independence, strategic thinking and refusing to be moved around as a pawn.

**Summer Skye:** Rain—the ordinary Oklahoma school song, changing seasons and the understanding that patterns in music need not be secret messages.

**Alexis Isabella-Jane:** The compass and seashell—direction, small memories and family history that survives noise.

**Maria:** Skyler's mother. She physically helps at the factory escape, brings food and practical support, but later admits she unwittingly completed a family-safety questionnaire circulated by a Society-front nonprofit. Her mistake hurts; she did not knowingly arrange abuse.

## The eight significant relationships — distinct people, distinct functions
Do *not* reduce the women to eight interchangeable losses. Give each a goal, a decision, a reason to leave or stay, and at least one moment not about Skyler. This is the proposed sequence; the author's chronology can be adjusted.

1. **Kaylee — first real love and family:** A sustained romance at the beginning. Their breakup is caused by grief, conflicting needs, his boundary mistakes, financial trouble and some later-proven outside pressure—not solely a villain pushing buttons.
2. **Tessa — coworker and lunch companion:** The ambiguous lunch relationship during late engagement. Their closeness feeds workplace rumors and Skyler must own crossing emotional boundaries, even though Vera later weaponizes the story.
3. **Hannah — the hopeful restart:** A kind, lively new partner after separation. An anonymous recording poisons trust, but Skyler's refusal to be transparent about what he actually knows matters too.
4. **Mei — the long-distance connection:** A Chinese-speaking student/engineer who challenges Skyler's assumptions. She helps identify that the supposedly rival US/China tracks share production artifacts. She is never treated as evidence of a foreign conspiracy.
5. **Ruby — the first dancer:** A smart, ambitious performer saving to open a studio. Attraction becomes a genuine friendship when he learns to see her beyond a stereotype. Their romance ends on boundaries, not punishment.
6. **Ivy — the second dancer:** She briefly moves in; they share tenderness and comic relief, but the crowded, unstable household magnifies his jealousy and Vera's rumors. Ivy makes a self-protective choice to leave.
7. **Haley — the later ex-fiancée and major betrayal:** A later serious partner, **not Kaylee**. Believing the Society's liaison can safeguard people she loves, she knowingly forwards personal screenshots and later learns they were used to manufacture charges. She must account for her choice and help establish the truth; redemption is earned, not automatic.
8. **June — the quiet future:** A librarian/community advocate who insists on normal boundaries and concrete proof. She is a slow-burn possibility late in the book, not an instant replacement for Kaylee or a guaranteed happy-ever-after.

**Important editorial choice:** Your request names both *Kaylee* and *Haley* as a former fiancée. This plan makes them **different fictional characters** so their roles and motivations remain clear. If you intended the same woman, combine them *before* prose rewriting and redesign Chapters 23 and 30. Never accidentally imply a real named co-parent committed a fictional crime.

## Allies and antagonists
**Lena:** Investigative reporter or civil-rights researcher with an ethical code. Challenges everything, including Skyler's theories, and insists on corroboration.

**Mara:** Former Mirror Room contractor, nicknamed Ghost. Helps Skyler survive the factory trap. Conceals her earlier part in designing a profiling test; earns trust by submitting evidence that incriminates herself.

**Reyes:** State/federal investigator with limited jurisdiction and no magical power. His strength is chain of custody, warrants and lawful follow-through.

**Caleb:** Colleague, comic relief and real friend. His job is to keep Skyler grounded, not to be secretly evil merely for a twist.

**Thorne:** Contract fixer who performs the cartel act and sells the $10 million deal. The visible villain and plausible red herring; ultimately subordinate to Dan but culpable in his own right.

**Dan Mercer:** Public hero, private architect. Generous, patient, personable, and devastating precisely because he always knows when Skyler needs rescuing. His key weakness: the legal paperwork required to move access and money leaves a trail he cannot edit without an independent custodian noticing.

## PROLOGUE — ROMEO, JULIET, AND THE WRONG LESSON
**Time:** Years before the main threat. **Target:** ~2,000 words.

At a school staging of *Romeo and Juliet*, young Skyler falls hard for the idea of love so powerful that the world conspires against it. A short, sweet, bittersweet romance surrounding the play ends not in death but in a banal separation: two people want different futures. He decides outsiders ruined it anyway. Later, at a community volunteering event, he meets Kaylee, whose laughter interrupts his rehearsed idea of tragic love. Closing image: he sets down a battered copy of the play to help her stack donated boxes. **Payoff:** At the ending, Skyler understands that love is not proven by persecution; it is proven by everyday care.

# 5. CONTINUITY / FAIRNESS RULES FOR THE REWRITE

1. **One canonical protagonist:** Skyler Blue throughout. **Kaylee** is the girls' mother; **Haley** is a distinct later partner; **Maria** is Skyler's mother; **Mara** is the former contractor. Change this only deliberately.
2. **Sallisaw is real, but institutions and conspirators are fictional.** Invent the school, church, private contractors, companies and named law-enforcement personnel. Dan Mercer is an invented retired director in an alternate-fictional FBI timeline, not a claim about a real director. Do not borrow the identity of a real official.
3. **No unverified real-person allegations as narrator fact.** If a character speculates about a billionaire, police leader, ex-partner or institution, identify it as suspicion and distinguish later proof. Seek legal review before publication for identifiable living people and the children.
4. **Eight relationship arcs, not eight sabotages.** Each partner retains agency. At least some breakups come from Skyler's choices or normal incompatibility; only specified communications are shown as manipulated after proof.
5. **What the technology can do:** compromise accounts through stolen permissions, repurpose metadata, alter scheduled posts, splice recordings, buy ad placement, impersonate a sender, and leave verifiable logs. **What it cannot do:** secretly control every phone, every song, all law enforcement, minds, or fate.
6. **The murder case remains fictional and legally coherent:** there is an investigation, a judge's decision and an unresolved reputational aftereffect; do not portray unverified “200 years” threats as genuine sentencing facts.
7. **Skyler makes repairable but meaningful mistakes:** emotional boundary crossing, poor security review, impulsive financial actions, anger and attempts to extract certainty from people. He must apologize and change, not simply be declared innocent of all harm.
8. **The criminal case has a readable chain:** the suspect/identity company → Grant's contract and Vale vendor → Mirror Room → Choir/Ledger/Pawns → Vera's grants → Dan's authorization. Each turn adds an independently corroborated link.
9. **Only one final Truth Protocol release:** draft/verification can happen earlier, public resolution belongs in Chapter 35.
10. **The children are not plot trophies or evidence packets:** no publishing their private messages or photos, no improbable child detective work, no need for Kaylee to remarry Skyler to signal safety.
11. **Each chapter earns its place:** a goal, an obstacle, a reversal, an emotional change and one decision that causes the next chapter. Cut any chapter that only restates fear.
12. **Narrative restraint makes the thriller scarier:** preserve two kinds of ambiguity—real crimes that take time to prove and unsettling coincidences that never become crimes.

# 6. DRAFTING & EDITORIAL TARGETS

| Dimension | Working target | How to assess it during the rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 112k–125k words | Track per-act and per-chapter word counts; no rushed final act |
| Opening | Hook by page 1; love/family established early | Test with 3–5 first readers |
| Pacing | One major irreversible reversal every 2–3 chapters | Scene cards and tension graph |
| Character arcs | Every major character has a desire, fear, secret and change | Character decision ledger |
| Romance | Tenderness, intimacy, conflict, repair across the book | Each romance scene changes a relationship or the case |
| Clues | 12–15 planted clues with fair payoffs | Foreshadowing tracker; eliminate arbitrary last-minute solutions |
| Hacking realism | Capabilities limited and logged | Technical beta read for plausibility; no magical interface |
| Legal realism | Credible warrants, trusts, custody and evidence | Legal sensitivity / plausibility review |
| Ending | Evidence resolves main plot; emotional arc resolves family safety | Reader should be able to articulate both victories |

**Suggested prose schedule:** Story beats → expanded 1–2-page synopsis for each chapter → scene cards → 112k–125k draft → developmental revision → line/copy edit → continuity and legal/privacy review → beta readers → proofread → publication files.
